Hero / landing. Your name, what you do in one line (like "Full-stack developer building Shopify apps and web platforms"), and a clear call to action (view work, contact). Don't make people scroll to figure out who you are.

About. Short, 3 to 4 sentences. Who you are, your main stack, what kind of problems you like solving. Skip the life story. A small photo helps people connect.

Skills / tech stack. Group them so they're scannable: frontend (React, HTML, CSS), backend (Node.js, Express, Laravel), databases (MongoDB, MySQL), and tools (Git, GraphQL, Docker, whatever applies). Don't list 40 things. List what you'd actually want to be hired for.

Projects. This is the part that matters most. For each project show: a title, a one-line description of what it does, the problem it solved, the stack used, your role, a screenshot or short demo, and two links (live demo and GitHub repo). 3 to 6 strong projects beat 15 weak ones. For something like your Shopify app work or the portfolio backend you built, describe the actual challenge ("built REST API with JWT auth, Cloudinary uploads, paginated queries") rather than just naming tech.

Experience. Brief work history if you have it. Company, role, what you worked on, dates. Keep it tight.

Contact. Email, LinkedIn, GitHub, and a simple contact form if you want. Make it easy to reach you

Resume. A downloadable PDF link. Recruiters look for this.
